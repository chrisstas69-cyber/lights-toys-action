### 3. Challenging One-Size-Fits-All Advice: Key Disagreements & Niche 
Dependencies

The sources reveal sharp operational disagreements where strategies conflicted

#### A. Daily Video Quantity: Extreme Volume (20/day) vs. Structured Volume 
(7/day) vs. Charisma Cap (3–4/day)
* **The Disagreement:** 
  * **Dami** insists on a minimum of **20 to 30 videos per day**, claiming high 
repetition is necessary to test hooks and maximize output [19-21].
  * **Linda** explicitly attempted 35–50 videos daily but abandoned it because 
it ruined her facial expressions and energy; she advocates strictly for **7 
high-quality videos daily** [22, 23, 82, 83].
  * **Margel** strongly warns *against* high output, capping her work at **3 to 
4 videos daily max** [84]. She states that beyond 4 or 5 videos, enthusiasm 
becomes forced and viewers immediately spot insincerity [84, 85].
* **Niche Context:** High volume (20+/day) suits rapid B-roll variations or 
faceless shoe/gadget reviews [21, 25], whereas lower volume (3–4/day) is 
essential when content relies heavily on personal charisma, vocal enthusiasm, 
and on-camera trust [51, 84-86].

#### B. Faceless Short Form vs. On-Camera Personal Branding
* **The Disagreement:** 
  * **Becca Lynn** built a \$184,000/month footwear business using **100% 
faceless videos**, noting that faceless content converts *better* in footwear 
because viewers don't feel like they are being aggressively sold to [87-92].
  * **Linda, Dami, and Margel** advocate for showing face and voice in every 
post to build cross-platform brand equity and prevent viewer churn [8, 49, 93, 
94].
  * **The AI Risk Tradeoff:** Becca Lynn acknowledges that faceless content is 
highly vulnerable to AI automation and is actively launching on-camera accounts 
to protect her long-term brand equity [80, 81, 95].
* **Niche Context:** Pure utility products (shoes, slippers, tools) convert 
effectively faceless because consumers care about function [89, 90]. Apparel, 
shapewear, and cosmetics rely heavily on body shape, relatable fit, and personal
trust [3, 58, 65, 66, 68, 96].

#### C. Single-Account Focus vs. Multi-Account Networks
* **The Disagreement:**
  * **Linda** tried managing multiple accounts, became overwhelmed, and 
consolidated all efforts into **one account**, scaling it to \$963,000 monthly 
GMV [97].
  * **Becca Lynn** maintains a **multi-account network** (8–10 videos/day on 
primary, 5/day on secondary, 2–5/day on beauty) to partition distinct product 
lines [24, 25, 81, 98, 99].
* **Niche Context:** Single-account execution works best when building a tight 
personal demographic brand (e.g., midsize fashion) [63, 97], while multi-account
strategies allow operators to cover non-overlapping verticals (e.g., footwear 
vs. cosmetics) without confusing the feed [24, 25, 81, 98].

#### D. Category Selection: High Commission Rate (Supplements) vs. Low 
Commission Volume (Fashion/Shoes)
* **The Trade-Off:** 
  * Beginners often chase high percentage payouts (30%–50% in 
supplements/wellness) [73].
  * **Linda** pursued supplements for months and earned only **\$1,500/month on 
\$10,000 GMV** (selling 1 item every 3–4 days) because she lacked passion and 
conversion friction was high [72, 73, 100].
  * **Margel and Becca Lynn** highlight that earning **\$1 to \$3 per item 
(15%–18%)** in fashion/footwear is far more lucrative; high order volume on 
everyday clothing scales into **\$500,000+ in net annual commission income** on 
\$5M gross sales [4, 35-37, 101, 102].

#### E. Live Streaming vs. Short Video Focus
* **The Trade-Off:**
  * **Noelie Fahas** generated over \$15 Million in sales primarily through 
**17-hour Mega Live events** (\$439,000 in a single live stream) [103, 104].
  * **Linda** does zero live broadcasts on her primary shop page, driving 
\$963,000/month strictly through **sub-30-second shoppable short videos** [22, 
97, 105, 106].
* **Niche Context:** High-touch, transformative garments like shapewear benefit 
from live before-and-after demonstrations and real-time Q&A [107-110], whereas 
standardized apparel and footwear convert rapidly in short, punchy video formats
[89, 90, 105, 106].
